# YouTube Alternatives for Creators in 2026 (12 Best Platforms Like YouTube)

YouTube is still the biggest video platform for creators, but it's far from the only one. In live streaming alone, viewers watched a record **31.4 billion hours** across platforms in Q2 2026, according to [Net Influencer's report on Streams Charts data](https://www.netinfluencer.com/q2-2026-livestreaming-sets-record-31-4b-hours-watched-as-world-cup-lifts-youtube/). YouTube took about **49.5%** of those hours, which means roughly half went to other platforms like Twitch, Kick and TikTok LIVE.

The video landscape is shifting in other ways too. Vimeo was bought by Bending Spoons in a USD 1.38 billion deal completed in November 2025, followed by major layoffs, according to [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/bending-spoons-acquires-vimeo-for-1-38b-triggers-mass-layoffs-and-closures). And YouTube's own Partner Program rules get stricter for new creators from February 2027.

That's why many creators are looking for an app like YouTube to reach new viewers, earn in different ways and avoid depending on one platform. In this guide, I'll compare the 12 best YouTube alternatives for creators in 2026, with what each one is best for, its pros and cons and how you can earn on it.

## Why Should Creators Look for YouTube Alternatives?

**YouTube alternatives help creators reach new audiences, earn in more ways and protect their business from changes on a single platform.**

Common reasons creators use other apps like YouTube:

- **New audiences:** Each platform has viewers who rarely use YouTube
- **Different formats:** Live streaming, short-form video or private, ad-free hosting
- **More income streams:** Subscriptions, tips, sponsorships and creator funds
- **Less risk:** A policy change, demonetization or algorithm shift on one platform hurts less
- **Different rules:** Some platforms suit niches or content styles YouTube doesn't favor

## What Are the Best YouTube Alternatives for Creators in 2026?

### 1. TikTok

**Best for: Fast reach with short-form video.**

TikTok's For You feed can show a new creator's video to huge audiences quickly. In the US, TikTok is now run by a US-owned joint venture set up in January 2026.

- **Pros:** Fast discovery, strong trends, TikTok Shop for selling
- **Cons:** Views fade quickly, US feed changes under the new ownership
- **Ways to earn:** Creator Rewards Program, LIVE gifts, subscriptions, TikTok Shop, brand deals

### 2. Instagram (Reels)

**Best for: Brands, lifestyle creators and local businesses.**

Instagram passed 3 billion monthly users in 2025, and more than half of time on the app goes to video.

- **Pros:** Huge audience, strong for community, DMs and sales
- **Cons:** Less direct ad revenue sharing for most creators
- **Ways to earn:** Brand partnerships, subscriptions, gifts, affiliate links, selling products

### 3. Facebook

**Best for: Longer videos, community groups and older audiences.**

Facebook supports Reels, long videos and live streams, and its content monetization program lets eligible creators earn from different content types.

- **Pros:** Large, broad audience and strong groups
- **Cons:** Reach can be unpredictable, younger audiences are smaller
- **Ways to earn:** Content monetization, stars, subscriptions, brand deals

### 4. Twitch

**Best for: Live streaming, gaming and real-time communities.**

Twitch is the best-known live streaming platform, with strong community tools like chat, raids and emotes.

- **Pros:** Loyal live communities, multiple ways to earn from fans
- **Cons:** Hard to get discovered, live-first rather than on-demand video
- **Ways to earn:** Subscriptions, Bits, ads, sponsorships

### 5. Kick

**Best for: Streamers looking for a growing, creator-friendly live platform.**

Kick has grown fast as a Twitch rival, with its hours watched rising 7.6% from Q1 to Q2 2026, according to the same Streams Charts data.

- **Pros:** Generous subscription revenue split, growing audience
- **Cons:** Smaller audience than Twitch and YouTube, content moderation concerns for some brands
- **Ways to earn:** Subscriptions, tips, creator incentive programs

### 6. Vimeo

**Best for: Professional, ad-free video hosting for businesses and filmmakers.**

Vimeo offers a clean, customizable player with no ads, privacy controls and tools for selling video. It's now owned by Bending Spoons, so watch for changes to plans and pricing.

- **Pros:** Ad-free player, privacy settings, professional look
- **Cons:** Paid plans for most features, small built-in audience
- **Ways to earn:** Selling videos, rentals and subscriptions through Vimeo OTT

### 7. Dailymotion

**Best for: A traditional YouTube like website with a global audience.**

Dailymotion is one of the oldest video sharing sites and works much like YouTube, with channels, playlists and on-demand video.

- **Pros:** Familiar format, works well for news, entertainment and sports clips
- **Cons:** Much smaller creator community than YouTube
- **Ways to earn:** Partner program ad revenue for eligible creators

### 8. Rumble

**Best for: Creators who want a YouTube-style platform with different content rules.**

Rumble is popular with news, commentary and political creators, and offers on-demand video and live streams.

- **Pros:** YouTube-like experience, licensing and creator deals
- **Cons:** Audience concentrated in certain niches, live hours watched fell 27% from Q1 to Q2 2026
- **Ways to earn:** Ad revenue, Rumble Rants, subscriptions, video licensing

### 9. Spotify (Video Podcasts)

**Best for: Podcasters who want to add video.**

Spotify now supports video podcasts, so creators can reach listeners and viewers in one app.

- **Pros:** Huge audience of podcast fans, audio and video in one place
- **Cons:** Built for podcasts, not general video
- **Ways to earn:** Spotify Partner Program (in eligible markets), subscriptions, sponsors

If you're starting there, this guide on how to [submit a podcast to Spotify](https://sites.google.com/view/submit-podcast-to-spotify/) walks through the setup step by step.

### 10. LinkedIn

**Best for: B2B creators, consultants and professional content.**

LinkedIn video works well for career advice, business tips and thought leadership.

- **Pros:** Professional audience, great for leads and clients
- **Cons:** Not built for entertainment or long-form channels
- **Ways to earn:** Indirectly, through clients, consulting, jobs and brand deals

### 11. Patreon

**Best for: Paid, members-only videos for loyal fans.**

Patreon lets creators upload videos directly and offer them to paying members.

- **Pros:** Direct fan income, no ads, more control
- **Cons:** No built-in discovery, so you need an audience elsewhere first
- **Ways to earn:** Monthly memberships, one-time purchases

### 12. PeerTube and Odysee

**Best for: Creators who want decentralized, independent video hosting.**

PeerTube is free, open-source software that lets anyone run their own video platform, while Odysee is a decentralized video site with its own community.

- **Pros:** More independence and control
- **Cons:** Small audiences and more technical setup, especially for PeerTube
- **Ways to earn:** Tips, donations and outside sponsorships

## Which YouTube Alternative Is Best for You?

**Choose based on the type of content you make and how you want to earn.**

The right platform like YouTube for you depends on your format:

- **Short-form video:** TikTok and Instagram Reels
- **Live streaming:** Twitch and Kick
- **Long-form, YouTube-style video:** Dailymotion, Rumble and Facebook
- **Professional or business video:** Vimeo and LinkedIn
- **Podcasts:** Spotify
- **Paid fan content:** Patreon
- **Independent hosting:** PeerTube and Odysee

## Are There Apps Similar to YouTube for Watching Videos?

**Yes. Apps similar to YouTube for viewers include TikTok, Instagram, Facebook Watch, Dailymotion, Vimeo, Twitch, Rumble and Spotify.**

Many viewers use several apps, depending on whether they want short videos, live streams, podcasts or long-form content. That's exactly why creators benefit from being present on more than one platform.

## Should You Leave YouTube Completely?

**No. For most creators, the smartest move is to keep YouTube and add one or two other platforms that suit your content.**

YouTube still has the biggest audience, the strongest search and clear ad revenue sharing. Other apps like YouTube work best as extra channels for reach, live streaming, community or direct fan income.

A simple plan:

1. **Keep YouTube** as your main long-form home.
2. **Add one short-form platform**, like TikTok or Instagram Reels, for discovery.
3. **Add one income platform**, like Patreon, or a live platform like Twitch, if it fits your content.
4. **Repurpose your best content** for each platform.

If you also make long recordings, like a video podcast, this comparison of [video podcasts vs audio podcasts](https://video-vs-audio-podcasts.mystrikingly.com/) shows how to turn one recording into several formats.

## What Mistakes Should You Avoid When Using YouTube Alternatives?

**Avoid spreading yourself too thin, posting identical content everywhere and ignoring each platform's rules.**

- **Joining too many platforms** at once
- **Reposting watermarked videos** from other apps
- **Ignoring platform culture**, like live interaction on Twitch
- **Chasing small platforms** with no audience in your niche
- **Not tracking results**, so you can't tell what works

## Final Thoughts

The best YouTube alternatives for creators in 2026 depend on your content and goals. TikTok and Instagram Reels are best for short-form reach, Twitch and Kick for live streaming, Vimeo and LinkedIn for professional video, Dailymotion and Rumble for a YouTube-style experience, Spotify for video podcasts and Patreon for direct fan income.

You don't need to leave YouTube. Keep it as your main home, add one or two platforms that fit your content and repurpose your best videos. That way, you reach more viewers, earn in more ways and protect your work from changes on any single platform.
